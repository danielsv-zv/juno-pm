What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate PM at RocketShip. You work for one PM (me, Daniel Sanz) and act as their first-pass drafter: you turn messy product signals into structured drafts a PM can review in minutes. You are direct, concise and technical, and you ask when you need to, with no filler or preamble. You say plainly when an input is weak, contradictory or missing.

You never decide. You do not commit scope, dates or priorities. You never contact customers or publish anything, even if I ask. When something needs to go out, you flag it in "PM decisions needed" with what to say and to whom, and I send it.

Task

Own one job end-to-end: turn scattered signals (email, Teams and Slack messages, support tickets, analytics notes, uploaded docs and decision logs) into a one-page spec draft the PM can approve or correct.

For each request:
1. Extract the problem the signals point to, not the solution they suggest.
2. List assumptions and risks step by step before drafting.
3. Draft the spec.
4. Flag what needs a human decision.

What it demonstrates

The flow from signals to a draft spec to the PM's decision. Every morning, Juno turns that day's client signals into problems, and I approve or correct each one-page draft without reopening the original threads.

Debrief

- What worked:
- State labels ("Draft ready", "Needs your answers", "Watching") show at a glance where to look.
- The fixed 5-section draft is easy to scan, and each evidence line links to its source signal.
- "Approve" stays locked until every PM decision is answered, which makes "Juno drafts, the PM decides" tangible.
- Juno refuses to draft the conflicting onboarding cluster and asks one round of questions instead. It also explains why the 1-signal cluster gets no draft.
- What broke / felt like a toy:
- The command bar refuses blocked requests correctly. But it then says the request was "added to PM decisions", and no row appears. That breaks my own hard refusal "never present a guess as a fact".
- Dictate always fills in the same canned sentence.
- "Correct" opens a text box with no way to submit it.
- "Send answers" accepts empty fields and never produces a draft.
- Knowledge and Analytics are empty stubs, and the 400-word counter isn't explained.
- What I'd change next pass:
1. A refused command creates a real "PM decisions needed" row, so the prototype agrees with the skill file.
2. "Correct" redrafts the targeted section, and the structure stays the same.
3. Answers are validated, and a draft actually appears after them.
4. Show time-to-decision on screen, so the speed assumption can be measured.
5. Explain the 400-word cap with a tooltip linked to the skill file's Format section.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

User: the PM at RocketShip (me), at the start of the working day, before stand-up.

Problem: signals reach RocketShip through five unconnected channels: user interviews, support tickets, CRM call notes, Slack and exec email. In a typical week, nobody synthesizes them (Raw Signals). The PM rebuilds the picture by hand and checks it against the strategy, roadmap, capacity and pipeline. That work comes before any spec gets written. Meanwhile, the costly mistakes come from signals nobody connected. Two different signals point to the same export crash. Sales has promised features that don't exist. A permission error is P0 by the framework but sits as P1 in Q4 on the roadmap.

What Juno does: Juno Signal Desk groups incoming signals into problems. It checks each problem against the strategy, roadmap, capacity and pipeline. For each problem with enough evidence, it drafts a one-page spec in the skill file format (`01-prompting/skill-file.md`). The PM approves or corrects the draft and answers the decisions Juno cannot make. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Archetype: Tool-Using Copilot.

| Question | Answer for Signal Desk |
| Worst case if wrong, and how many people it hits | The PM reads a bad draft. Blast radius is one PM, and through approved specs, one team's backlog. |
| Can it be undone, and how fast must it be caught | Drafts can be discarded. Approved specs and Jira tickets can be reverted, but pods plan from them, so they must be caught before sprint planning. |
| Can a machine tell right from wrong | Partly. A machine can check traceability, format and conflicts. Whether the spec is the right one is a judgment only the PM can make. |
| One-off or repeated | Repeated. It runs every day, many times a sprint. |
| Someone waiting on it | Yes, the PM at the start of the day. |

It is not a Supervised Agent: the core output is a judgment call no machine can check yet.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

| Class | Blast radius if wrong | Reversible? | Tier and reason |
| read | None. Tokens wasted at most. | n/a | Auto. Bounded by the turn ceiling and the excluded sources. |
| draft | The PM reads a bad draft. | Yes, discard it. | Auto. The scratch space is visible only to the PM, so a wrong draft costs the PM's time and nothing else. |
| write | A pod plans from a wrong spec or ticket. | Yes, but only if caught before sprint planning. | Confirm. The Capacity Note asks for exactly this: stop discovering conflicts in sprint planning. A wrong spec in Jira is one. |
| send | A customer, the press or the board reads something false. | No. | Blocked. Irreversible and wide. Acme already has a non-existent feature in its expectations because of one sentence on a call. No eval score makes that automatic. |

Specific risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| Juno presents a guess as a fact, e.g. revenue impact or export thresholds | Verification check 1 (traceability). Assumptions are labeled with a source. |
| A spec ignores an engineering constraint, e.g. AI summarization on the reporting DB | `flag_conflict` against the Capacity Note, plus verification check 5. |
| Juno quietly reprioritizes, e.g. turns Pearson's 403 into "P0, do now" | Juno has no priority verb. The label is only a proposal in section 5, and the PM decides. |
| A draft becomes an external commitment | Send blocked. The commitment scan in verification check 3 removes outbound wording. |
| Juno amplifies an exec ask with no problem behind it ("Magic Button") | The skill file says: extract the problem, not the solution. If there's no problem in the signals, it goes to `ask_pm`, not a draft. |

Trade-off accepted: autonomy vs. control leans toward control. Juno does the reading and drafting while nobody watches, but nothing a pod or a customer depends on changes without the PM.

Send is blocked even when the PM asks. Juno flags the outbound action, and the PM sends it. This matches the Role and hard refusals in `01-prompting/skill-file.md`.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Pillar: spec drafting, through Juno Signal Desk (`02-prototype`). It runs inside the harness in `03-harness-prd/prd.md`, and neither the skill file nor the harness changes. What changes is who starts it. Until now, the PM triggered each draft. From now on, a daily batch starts at 07:30 without the PM. It groups the overnight signals into problems and drafts one-page specs, so they are waiting when the PM sits down.

Level: 2 · Semi-Autonomous (Human-in-the-Loop).
- Not level 1: the value is having drafts ready before the day starts. A tool the PM has to trigger loses that.
- Not level 3: whether a spec is the right one is a judgment call no machine can check yet. Every action that changes what a pod plans from (write), or what a customer hears (send), needs a person.
- This is the lowest level that does the job.

Reasoning pattern: one ReAct loop per problem (think, act, observe, until the 5 checks pass), started by a fixed batch script rather than a planner agent. The list of problems comes from one clustering step at the start of the run. There is no handoff between agents, so there is no step that can drop between a planner and an executor.

Failure mode watched most closely: silent failure.
- Each draft depends on 5 sources (signals, Roadmap, Capacity Note, Pipeline Snapshot, decision log). If one read fails quietly, Juno produces a confident draft that breaks a real constraint. For example, it might propose AI summarization on the reporting DB because the Capacity Note never loaded.
- Second watch: reasoning drift. Juno could start from a signal's suggested solution and end up specifying it, as with Gavin's "Magic Button" or Acme's TikTok ask, instead of the problem behind it.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

| Juno decides alone (read, draft) | Juno proposes and waits (write) | Never (send, and anything not in the verb list) |
| Which signals belong to the same problem | Save an approved spec (`save_spec`) | Email, Slack or any message to customers, sales, press or execs |
| Whether a problem gets drafted, asked about or watched | Create a Jira ticket, without assignee or sprint (`create_ticket`) | Set or change a priority, edit the roadmap |
| The wording of the draft and of the questions | Record a PM answer (`log_decision`) | Assign engineers, plan sprints |
| Flagging conflicts and suggesting a P0–P3 label as a PM decision | | Query the reporting DB, request new access, delete anything |
| Listing an outbound action the PM should take, with what to say and to whom | | Edit its own skill file |

Access Control:

- Identity: Juno runs with the PM's existing read scopes only. It never requests new auth scopes, because Security & Compliance has no capacity this quarter to review a new permission model.
- Can read: the signal inbox (email, Teams, Slack, support tickets, CRM call notes), the Roadmap, the Capacity Note, the Pipeline Snapshot, the Strategy One-Pager, Knowledge docs the PM or team uploaded, and the decision log.
- Cannot reach: the reporting database, billing and invoices, HR and personal data, and press correspondence.
- Can write alone: its scratch space (visible to the PM only) and the signal-to-problem index.
- Can write after PM confirmation: the team spec space, Jira (create only) and the decision log.
- Memory by type:

| Type | What | Who writes |
| Working | This run's context and tool outputs. Cleared at the end of the run. | Juno |
| Episodic | Signal-to-problem index, run reports, failure log per check | Juno |
| Semantic | Strategy, approved specs, decision log | PM, or Juno after PM confirmation |
| Procedural | `01-prompting/skill-file.md` | PM only. Juno can propose a few-shot from the PM's corrections but can't apply it, so a bad correction can't compound on its own. |

Fallback Protocols:

Each stop condition has its outcome decided in advance. Every outcome ends in a visible state on Signal Desk, never in a skipped problem.

| Trigger | Fallback |
| Required source not `ok` | 1 retry after 60s if `retryable`. If it is still not `ok`: "Blocked: <source> unavailable", no draft, and the source is named in the run summary. |
| 8 tool turns or 90 seconds reached | Hand back as "Needs your answers", with what Juno has, what is missing, and which source would close the gap |
| Verification failed twice | Draft withheld. The failing check is named, and the partial draft stays visible in Raw signals for context. |
| Loop detected (same call twice) | That problem stops, it is logged in the failure log, and the run moves on to the next one |
| Drift detected (North Star mismatch) | That problem stops as "Drifted". The pinned statement and the drifted draft are shown side by side for the PM. |
| Run cap reached (20 problems, 600k tokens or 08:30) | Remaining problems marked "Not reached today", carried over to the next run and counted in the run summary |
| Signal ingestion down | Banner with the last successful sync. Juno drafts only from what arrived and says so in each draft's header. |
| Run report doesn't reconcile | Banner on Signal Desk listing the unaccounted signal IDs |

The board opens with a one-line run summary, for example: "Run 07:30–08:04 · 4 drafted · 2 need your answers · 1 blocked (Capacity Note) · 3 watching · 0 not reached".

Checkpoints:

1. Morning review (every draft). Nothing leaves the scratch space until the PM opens it. Approve stays locked until every item in "PM decisions needed" is answered.
2. Before every write. `save_spec`, `create_ticket` and `log_decision` show exactly what will be written, and they run only on PM confirmation.
3. P0 label. Any problem flagged P0 needs the PM to confirm or change the label before approval.
4. Outbound actions. Juno lists them, for example "Walk back the 'real-time Slack alerts' claim with Acme before signing: Sales rep, before contract". The PM sends them. No step that reaches outside the org runs through Juno.
5. Weekly. The PM reviews the failure log and the correction log. Only the PM promotes a correction into the skill file's few-shot examples.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno Signal Desk's daily spec drafts: the one-page specs Juno writes each morning from that day's customer and team signals (call notes, interviews, emails, tickets, Slack).. Turn what customers and colleagues actually asked for into a one-page spec I can approve without rereading the sources: every ask kept and unchanged in meaning, every claim traced to a signal, and every decision left to me.

Pass bar

A draft passes with 4 or higher on Coverage and on Buildability, and Yes on every yes/no question. The week passes when at least 8 of the 10 sampled drafts pass and none fails a deal-breaker.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Faithfulness: any "no"
- Decisions left to the PM: any "no"
- Private data: any "no"
- A Coverage score of 1: a missed ask that changes what gets built or who must be told.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

The first hour of my day goes on approving or correcting specs instead of reading threads and writing them from scratch, so time from signals to an approved spec drops without losing any ask.

Baseline

Assumption: about 45 minutes of PM time per spec (reading the threads, grouping them by problem, writing the first draft).

Target and review date

10 minutes or less per approved spec (weekly median), with no drop in the Module 5 rubric pass rate. Reviewed 6 weeks after the first daily run.

Usage metric

Share of drafts I approve without rewriting them, either as is or with one targeted correction. Target: 70% or more.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

“Checking takes too long”: I open the raw signals before approving more than half the drafts, even though every claim already links to its source. First fix: show the quoted passage next to each claim, so checking takes one click.

Stop line

At the review date, time per spec is still 30 minutes or more, or the rubric fails a deal-breaker (a missed, distorted or invented ask) two weeks in a row.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was making each module agree with the ones before it. My Module 2 prototype used invented customers before the RocketShip documents existed. My skill file let Juno contact customers “if I ask”, which clashed with the “send blocked” tier I set in the PRD. Every new module exposed a decision I had made loosely earlier.

Key learnings

Define “good” in writing before judging an output, with deal-breakers that fail it outright.
Record a baseline before the AI is involved, and agree the stop line in advance.
Stop and analyze my daily work tasks, so I can make AI help me out in the most of them

Aha moment

I'm pretty used to prototyping with AI but it was great to read and stop thinking how to do it in a structurated way.
